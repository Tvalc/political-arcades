A guide to Greatest Hits

Sick Burns

The delivery is part of the story.

A political comeback compresses a contrast into a line people remember. This shelf collects that performance: the pause before a reply, a contradiction made visible or a question an opponent struggles to answer. It is a comedy and rhetoric collection, not a fact-check scorecard.

Why a comeback lands

A sharp reply usually depends on the setup. Watch who is being answered and what they said first. A line that sounds decisive in isolation can be doing something narrower in the exchange: challenging credibility, changing the subject or refusing a premise.

Separate the laugh from the evidence. Delivery can make an assertion feel proved before the listener has considered it. When the clip concerns a vote, donation or public record, the joke is not a substitute for checking that record. Our labels describe the moment; they do not settle the dispute.

Publication dates and campaign edits matter. A campaign highlight is selected by somebody trying to persuade you. We retain the publisher attribution and link back to the upload so that the source of that selection remains visible.

Watch the differences

The visual gag versus the moral reversal

A yacht with a yacht makes scale absurd. A response to a religious attack contests who gets to define the moral standard. Both compress a contrast, but they ask the audience to do different work. Identify the premise each punchline needs you to accept.

The campaign montage versus the broadcaster exchange

Watch how the impression changes when a campaign selects an opponent’s moments and when a broadcaster presents an exchange with a reply. Neither edit is the full debate. The useful comparison is what setup and response each publisher leaves available to judge.

Cuomo, in the campaign’s cutting room · Billionaires, Trump, and the debate stage

The missing answer versus the follow-up question

An unanswered call turns absence into the whole joke. Hearing questions can instead press toward records and witnesses that might support a claim. They are different forms of accountability: do not confuse the campaign’s presentation of silence with evidence obtained in testimony.

When the hero takes the joke

The roast makes the candidate a target; debate bingo makes the supporters an audience with a game of their own. These are examples of campaign comedy beyond simply insulting a rival. Compare who is invited to laugh, and at whose expense.
